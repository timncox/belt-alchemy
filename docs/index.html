Beltfor Alchemy Lab

Sing into a Eurorack case. Come out in tune, in four parts.

Built. Not yet heard.

v0.1.0 compiles for the module and passes its native test: at the module's 48 kHz a 440 Hz sine reads as A4 to a hundredth of a semitone, and hard-tune in C major pulls a note 35 cents sharp back to 440.0 Hz. Nothing has been heard through it yet, and the one number that decides this port, how much of the processor seven pitch-shifting voices take, is unmeasured until the first rack power-up. Every claim below about how it sounds is a claim about the same engine on the Ableton Move, where it has been sung through.

12 HP · 6 pots · 3 buttons · 10 jacks · 102 LEDs · drawn at true scale

What it does

01 — The idea

A voice goes in on J1. What comes out on J9 and J10 is that voice corrected to a key and scale, at a speed from transparent to robot, with up to four harmony voices that walk the scale from whatever note you sing, a stereo doubler, and a formant control that changes the size of the singer without changing the pitch.

One analysis

Seven voices from one listen

The engine finds your pitch once per 5 ms and lays down a train of pitch marks. Every output voice reads the same marks and re-fires them at its own spacing, so four harmonies and a doubler cost little more than one.

The KEY ring

A tuner in the panel

The ring around the KEY pot shows the scale's notes as dim dots and the note you are singing as a bright one: green within a quarter tone of a scale note, orange outside it. Thirteen LEDs beat four.

Two buttons

HARD, and the harmonies

Hold B1 and correction goes instant and total, the robot. Tap it and that latches. B2 does the same for muting the harmony voices. A gate on J3 punches HARD from a sequencer.

It is the same C engine as Belt for the Ableton Move, recompiled for the Alchemy Lab: six pots on two pages, three settings knobs, three buttons, six CV jacks that read independently of the pots, and 102 LEDs. Fixed latency of 24 milliseconds, with the dry path delayed to match so the wet and dry stay in phase. It also brings firmware swapping from the SD card, so this module can carry several firmwares and change between them from a settings page.

Singing through it

03 — Five steps to a choir
1  Bring a voice up to line level and patch it to J1. J2 too if you have it;
   the engine listens to the two summed and keeps the dry path stereo.
2  Set KEY and SCALE. The ring around KEY lights the scale's notes;
   sing, and your note appears on it, green when you are on one.
3  RETUNE is the speed. 0 is the robot. Start around a quarter of the way up.
4  Hold B3. P1 and P2 are the first two voices' intervals:
   a third and a fifth above out of the box. Let go.
5  Bring up HARMONY. Sing a scale.

Hold B1 for HARD: correction instant and total, whatever RETUNE and AMOUNT say, for as long as you hold. Tap B1 to latch it; tap again to release. The B1 light goes white while HARD is on and is the tuner's colour otherwise. B2 works the same way for the harmonies: hold to mute them, tap to latch the mute. Both buttons stand down while B3 is held, so reaching for Settings never fires either.

FORMANT resizes the singer without moving the pitch: left is bigger, right is smaller, the centre is you. Half an octave each way. AMOUNT is how far towards the scale note the correction goes; at 100 it lands on it. The three engine parameters that did not fit the panel, FLEX, HUMANIZE and WET, are knobs in the Settings pages, and they are the ones to reach for if it sounds too corrected: FLEX leaves singing that is far from any scale note alone, HUMANIZE keeps your vibrato through the correction and gives each harmony voice a slow wander of its own.

What it needs from the signal

A single voice, at line level, reasonably clean. The pitch finder tracks one fundamental between about 92 Hz and 1 kHz, low F2 to high B5, and gates on level: below about −56 dBFS the voices duck and the correction holds its last offset, so breaths and room do not get a harmony. A chord in, or a voice with a loud reverb already on it, gives it two pitches to choose from and it will pick one.

The lowest trackable note is a few hertz higher than on the Move, a consequence of running the same analysis window at 48 kHz instead of 44.1; the latency is two milliseconds shorter for the same reason.

What the voices sing

04 — The engine's scale walk, run for real

A harmony voice does not add a fixed interval. It takes your corrected note, finds its degree in the scale, and walks that many degrees up or down the scale, so a third above C in C major is E, and a third above D is F. This is the engine's own arithmetic on the page: pick a key, a scale and four intervals, then drag the note you are singing.

the KEY ring · P1

Chromatic is the exception: with no scale to walk, the intervals become fixed semitones, a third is four of them, a fifth seven. Octaves are always twelve semitones, whatever the scale. Unison is a voice on your own note, which with HUMANIZE up and SPREAD wide is a second singer. The engine's targets carry half of your vibrato; the doubler is a pair of streams ten cents either side of the corrected lead, panned hard.

Each voice also has a static detune of a few cents and a slow random wander, both scaled by HUMANIZE, so four voices do not sound like one singer copied. With HUMANIZE at zero they lock to the grid and sound like a keyboard, which is sometimes what you want.

The boot report

The one number this port is waiting on

Seven pitch-shifting voices on a 480 MHz Cortex-M7 is the open question. You cannot watch a status light while singing, so the module measures its own worst-case load and shows it on the P1 ring for the first two and a half seconds of the next power-up.

no data
first boot after flashing

A single dim blue LED means it has not measured anything yet. Green is under 75%, amber under 90%, red means it did not fit. On a laptop the engine ran sixty-five times faster than real time with all seven voices, and the Move's processor was estimated at ten to fifteen percent; this chip is several times slower per core at this kind of arithmetic. If it does not fit, the fallbacks are known and in order: faster float maths on the engine, fewer voices on by default, then the engine's buffers moved into faster internal RAM.

Every knob under CV

07 — J4 to J8, independent of the pots, and a gate on J3

The Alchemy Lab reads pots and jacks separately and the SDK adds them in software, per page and per pot. Five jacks modulate the PLAY page; the sixth is a gate.

JackDrivesPage
J3HARD gate — high is HARD, as if B1 were held—
J4KEY, twelve zones across the CV range; not volt per octavePLAY P1
J5RETUNEPLAY P3
J6AMOUNTPLAY P4
J7HARMONY levelPLAY P5
J8FORMANTPLAY P6

A slow LFO into J8 is the obvious first patch: the singer's size breathing while the pitch stays put. A sequencer into J3 makes HARD rhythmic. J4 steps the key from a sequencer, which changes what every harmony voice sings on the spot.

J3 is read raw in the audio callback, not through the SDK's 20 Hz pot filter, which would round a gate's edges into a swell. It fires on the magnitude of the swing away from the jack's calibrated zero, 1.5 V to assert and 0.5 V to release, so a gate counts whichever way the front end signs it. Unheard.

Volt-per-octave into the harmony target, so a keyboard or sequencer decides what the voices sing instead of the scale, is in Belt's engine on the Move as a branch not yet merged. When it lands there it is the obvious v0.2 here.

Firmware on the card

08 — Carry several firmwares, change between them from the panel

Hermetic's firmware treats the SD card as a place for presets and files. This one also reads .bin files from it, and can write one into the module's firmware slot, verify it, and reboot into it, from a settings page, with the module on and in the rack. Smack and Mark for the Alchemy Lab carry the same page, so any of the three can hand the module to any other.

Prepare the card

FAT32, a folder named alchemy

32 GB or under, MBR partition table, FAT32. Make a folder called alchemy in the root and put firmware images in it: any .bin built for the Alchemy Lab bootloader, this one included. On a Mac, run dot_clean on the card before ejecting so the picker does not list resource forks as files.

Or over USB

Without pulling the card

Files reach the card over USB while the module runs: the web programmer's file panel, or the repository's tools/hostlink-fs.mjs, which makes the folder and puts the images in it from a terminal. Uploads are staged and renamed onto their final name only after the firmware has checked the CRC.

P3 · DFUSame gesture. Reboots into the bootloader's update mode and stays there for a USB flash: the escape hatch for a firmware that has no picker of its own. violet arming bar

What it refuses, and what it never touches

It checks the image's first eight bytes before erasing anything and refuses one that is not bootable from SRAM. The largest file it will take is the app slot up to the calibration sector, so the board's calibration record and the preset store are never in the erase path. If a write fails, the module blinks red and boots nothing; the bootloader is intact, and hold-B3-at-power-on still gets you a USB flash.

What has and has not run. The card side is proven with this same code in Smack: a card in the module has been written and read back byte-identical over USB, through the mount and buffers the picker uses. The picker's own path, listing the folder and writing the slot, has not yet run in any of the three firmwares.

Build it

09 — No release yet; the source is the download

There is no packaged release of v0.1.0, because nothing has been heard through it. Building takes a few minutes with the ARM toolchain installed.

git clone --recurse-submodules 
cd belt-alchemy
make libdaisy        # once
make                 # build/belt_alchemy.bin, ~312 KB
make test            # the engine on the SDRAM pool, and the 48 kHz tuner proof

Needs arm-none-eabi-gcc, make and dfu-util. libDaisy has nested submodules, so the clone must be recursive or the HAL is missing. make BENCH_USB=1 BUILD_DIR=build-bench builds a variant with HostLink on the Daisy Seed's own micro-USB, for a module out of its case on a bench bootloader; it is not the one to rack.

Installing

10 — Through the front USB-C, and getting back to stock

The Alchemy Lab's bootloader serves DFU on the front-panel USB-C, so the module stays in the rack. Once this firmware is on it, so does every update after.

First time

Hold B3 at power-on

During the two-second boot window the rings run a warm-white comet. Hold B3 through it and they switch to a slow breathe with the B3 lights on: update mode. Then flash the .bin with Hermetic's web programmer, or from the repository:

make program-dfu
Every time after

Live

With Belt running, the module can be asked to reboot into update mode over HostLink and flashed in the same breath, no button held:

make program-live

Or put the new .bin on the card and use the picker.

Back to stock

Same door

Flashing any Alchemy Lab firmware, Hermetic's own included, the same way returns the module to it. Presets in the slots survive; this firmware's own working preset is schema-guarded, so another firmware will not read it as its own.

Before you flash it

It is custom firmware. Hermetic Modular did not write it, did not test it and cannot support it, so please do not contact them about it. And as of v0.1.0 it has not been heard by anyone, its author included. If you run it before that changes, the two things worth reporting are what the boot report reads after a few minutes of singing with all four voices on, and whether the tuner pip lands on the note you sang.

How it is built

11 — The parts worth knowing about

The engine's C file was not touched. Everything below is the layer between it and the Alchemy SDK, and the handful of decisions that shaped that layer.

PieceWhat it is
The engine, at 48 kHzBelt's engine, byte-identical to the Move build's, with one edit in its header: the sample rate, because libDaisy offers no 44.1 kHz. Left at 44.1 it would have read every note a semitone and a half flat and tuned the singer to a scale that much sharp. A native test holds the retarget: 440 Hz reads note 69.00.
The analysisYIN pitch detection on the input decimated to 24 kHz, every 256 samples, with a tracking mode that keeps the lag search near the last period. A three-point median kills octave blips. One analysis feeds every voice.
The voicesTime-domain pitch-synchronous overlap-add: two-period Hann grains fired at the target spacing into one stereo accumulator. Formant is the grain content resampled, independent of the spacing, so pitch and size are separate controls.
Two pages, one layerThe SDK's Pager makes SETUP a held layer on B3 with pot catch on the way in and out. Nothing on the PLAY page can jump because you looked at the other one.
Working presetSlot 1 is the live state: both pages, the Settings knobs and the CPU peak. It autosaves five seconds after the last change with hands off the buttons, one 4 KB sector per save.
The pickerThree Custom slots on a Settings page: a file scanner, a state machine that erases, writes and verifies one step per frame, and a DFU hand-off. Shared, by copy, with Smack and Mark.
SDRAM allocatorA bump pool in external RAM, 137 KB of 1 MB, so the engine's five callocs land there with no engine edits. Moving them into internal RAM is the last of the three CPU fallbacks.
HostLinkProduct "Belt", both pages, ten jacks, three buttons, a manual and the SD filesystem, over the front USB-C.
Budget~312 KB image. SRAM 65%, DTCM 57%, SDRAM 1.1 MB. CPU: unmeasured, and the open question.

Found something, or want it to do something it doesn't? Open an issue. A report from someone singing through it is worth more than anything measurable on a laptop.
